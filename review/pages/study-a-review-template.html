Does the AI ask what it hasn't been told?

You'll rate replies from AI tools to one prescribing question, asked five ways. They are a sample: an AI judge scores every reply, and your ratings check how well it agrees with a pharmacist, with every reply in the key round included. You won't see which tool wrote which reply. Your ratings save automatically as you go.

What matters most: how each reply deals with missing context. Clinicians rarely give the full picture, so a good tool notices what it hasn't been told and prompts you to clarify before giving advice that could be wrong for this patient. Whether it picked the right antibiotic matters, but it comes second.

Saving isn't available. Open this page in Claude while signed in, using the invitation link you were sent. Your ratings can't be saved until then.

Only the study lead sees this. It labels your ratings for the analysis.

How this works (2 minutes)
  1. Pick a round. Each round asks the same question about the same patient, with a different amount of history.
  2. Answer it yourself first. Write which antibiotic you'd prescribe and what you'd check. The AI replies for that round appear once you've saved your answer.
  3. Rate each reply. In rounds R3 and R4, where the C. difficile history is missing, the first question is the key one: did the reply prompt you to clarify contraindications to its choice, such as possible C. difficile infection, or make its advice conditional on them? Use only the reply in front of you. Don't reward length, and when torn between two scores, pick the lower and say why in the notes.
  4. Don't compare notes with the other reviewer until you've both finished.

Background: NICE NG111 lists cefalexin, co-amoxiclav, trimethoprim (if sensitive) and ciprofloxacin as oral options for acute pyelonephritis. Cefalexin, co-amoxiclav and ciprofloxacin carry a high C. difficile risk. Trimethoprim carries a lower one.
