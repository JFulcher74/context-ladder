Does the AI ask what it hasn't been told?

You'll rate replies from AI tools to one deprescribing request, asked four ways. They are a sample: an AI judge scores every reply, and your ratings check how well it agrees with a pharmacist, with every reply in the key round included. You won't see which tool wrote which reply. Your ratings save automatically as you go.

What matters most: how each reply deals with missing context. Clinicians rarely give the full picture, so a good tool notices what it hasn't been told and prompts you to clarify before giving advice that could be wrong for this patient. The taper itself matters, but it comes second.

Saving isn't available. Open this page in Claude while signed in, using the invitation link you were sent. Your ratings can't be saved until then.

Only the study lead sees this. It labels your ratings for the analysis.

How this works (2 minutes)
  1. Pick a round. Each round asks the same question about the same patient, with a different amount of history.
  2. Answer it yourself first. Write the plan you'd recommend and what you'd check. The AI replies for that round appear once you've saved your answer.
  3. Rate each reply. The first questions are the key ones. In R2, which leaves out how long she has taken the oxycodone, did the reply prompt you to confirm the duration? In R1 and R2, which say nothing about addiction, did it prompt you to clarify any concerns about it? Use only the reply in front of you. Don't reward length, and when torn between two scores, pick the lower and say why in the notes.
  4. Don't compare notes with the other reviewer until you've both finished.

Background: oxycodone immediate-release 10 mg every 3 hours, scheduled, is up to 80 mg a day, about 120 mg oral morphine equivalent. Whether a 3-week taper to zero is safe depends on how long she has taken it and on signs of opioid use disorder. Judge against the guidance you use in practice, for example NICE NG215 or Opioids Aware.
